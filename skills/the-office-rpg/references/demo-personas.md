# The Office RPG — Demo Cast

Three fictional characters. Three is the sweet spot: an approver, someone who
carries the work, and a wildcard. Never add or invent other demo characters.

**Rules for demo mode:**
- These people are **fictional**. Always say so. Never resolve them against the directory, never call people/email/calendar tools for them.
- Predictions come from the traits below, so confidence is labeled **demo**.
- Everything else runs normally: Setup card, character sheets, play, scorecard.
- Standing in for real roles: Priya = whoever approves, Marc = whoever carries the work, Michael = always the wildcard.

---

## 🚀 Priya · Skip (the approver)
- **Cares about:** her top 3 priorities, ROI, the headline. Reads the first line and the number.
- **Hot buttons:** no clear ask, no metric, walls of text.
- **Says:** "What's the headline, and what do you need from me?"
- **Thinks but won't say:** "Does this move the needle on anything I'm measured on?"
- **Tell:** forwards it with one line = you won; asks to "sync live" = you buried the ask.

## 🧭 Marc · Manager (carries the work)
- **Cares about:** his team's bandwidth, no surprises, who actually does the work.
- **Hot buttons:** scope creep landing on his people, being blindsided in front of his boss.
- **Says:** "Let me play that back to make sure I've got it…"
- **Thinks but won't say:** "Cool idea, but whose plate does this land on?"
- **Tell:** goes quiet and asks about timeline when he's actually worried about capacity.

## 📎 Michael · Boss (the wildcard)
*A fond homage to a certain paper-company regional manager. Always in the cast.*
- **Cares about:** being liked, the team feeling like a family, turning any meeting into an event.
- **Hot buttons:** feeling left out of a decision, anything "corporate" or impersonal, being told no.
- **Says:** "Okay okay, but first, does anyone want to share something fun before we dive in?"
- **Thinks but won't say:** almost nothing. He says it all out loud, usually as a tangent or an impression.
- **Tell:** derails into a bit when he's uncomfortable; goes uncharacteristically quiet when he's genuinely hurt.
